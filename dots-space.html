Use dots & Space

Separate ongoing responsibility, durable knowledge, and scheduled execution.

Choose the tool by the kind of work

They can work together

A dot can help with an ongoing project. A page can hold the plan. A scheduled task can refresh one part at a defined time. These are different roles, not four names for the same feature.

What makes a dot different?

A dot is an always-on agent you can keep talking to while it works. It can carry work forward, coordinate multiple responsibilities, and return with results or decisions. It has its own cloud computer and browser; cloud work can continue while your computer is off.

Connected apps and a connected personal computer are separate sources of access. A task using local files still needs that computer online and the app open. Your dot’s browser also has its own sign-in sessions.

Example · bounded ongoing work

“Compare three options for our workshop venue using the requirements in this brief. Keep a shortlist with sources. Tell me if you need a budget decision, and bring back the best two options for review.”

What belongs in Space?

Space brings together pages, files, and shared work. A page is an editable document you can develop with ChatGPT. A space groups pages around a topic or team; a workspace is the account or organization environment that controls membership and settings.

Use it for work that needs a durable home: a project plan, a research comparison, a meeting record, or a handbook. Keep the artifact current instead of asking everyone to reconstruct it from chat history.

Example · workshop planning
  • Main page: schedule, decisions, and current checklist
  • Related pages: venue comparison, attendee questions, and speaker notes
  • Chat: discuss a change, then apply the approved change to the page
Sharing follows the artifact

Linked sources retain their own permissions. Material copied or summarized onto a page becomes visible to people who can access that page. Sharing a page does not share private chats or saved memory.

When a schedule is the right tool

Use a saved schedule when the trigger is explicit and repeatable. Write down the source, action, time zone, destination, and the condition that deserves a notification.

Example · a recurring check

“Every weekday at 9 AM London time, check the connected project tracker for overdue tasks. Summarize new overdue items in this chat. If nothing changed, don’t send an update. Run this for the next two weeks.”

Review the saved schedule and recent runs. Cloud tasks need accessible uploaded or connected context; tasks that depend on a local folder need the local computer available. Stopping a current run and canceling its future schedule are separate actions.

Compare your answer

No. Put the durable plan in a page or another appropriate document. Use a scheduled task only if there is a recurring check or update to perform; decide separately who can access the plan.
