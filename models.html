Choose a model

Choose a model for the job, then decide how much thinking time and speed are worth paying for.

Three controls: model, effort, and speed

Model = the engine

Choose the model for the job. A small text edit may need less capability than debugging an unfamiliar codebase.

Effort = room to think

Effort controls how much reasoning the model can use. More effort can help with a difficult problem, but usually takes more time and tokens. You still need to check the answer.

Speed = a service tier, not extra intelligence

Standard, Fast, and Astra Ultrafast change the speed/usage trade-off. Keep model and effort separate: faster token generation does not mean a harder-thinking model or faster tools.

Three GPT starting points for this class

The official model-selection guide informs the task roles below. These starting presets are our course judgment; the broad intelligence score is not a coding ranking.

Default: Sol Medium

Scoped features and everyday work. GPT-6.1 Sol medium scores 48 at $0.21 per benchmark task. Give it relevant files and a clear definition of done before adding more reasoning.

Escalate: Sol Xhigh

Difficult bugs and verification. Sol xhigh reaches 51 at $0.39. Use it when medium misses a specific edge case. It is a smaller cost step than jumping straight to Astra.

Quality-first: Astra Medium

Broad context, ambiguity, and multi-step work. Official guidance positions Astra for demanding, multi-step work; Medium is our suggested quality-first starting point. Consider Max when extra depth matters more than time or usage, or as another attempt on a hard issue.

Where Luna fits

Luna max is the low-cost comparison: 37 at $0.07. Use small, easy-to-review tasks to see whether its quality is enough. For the pet demo, Luna Medium remains a suggested rehearsal setting, not a proven optimum.

Claude, Gemini, and Grok remain useful comparison points. The presets above are GPT-only because this is a Codex class; the benchmark is not a ranking of complete agent products.

How to interpret “token spending speed”

Same work: the billing multiplier changes the charge; it does not mean the model must produce 8× as many tokens. Actual token counts still depend on context, effort, output, retries, and tool use.

Per minute: for a constant token mix and sustained generation, relative burn rate ≈ generation-speed multiplier × usage multiplier. At the full 8× Astra claim, that is 8 × 8 = 64× included allowance, or 8 × 6 = 48× paid-credit usage per generation minute. These are illustrative ratios, not predictions of your dashboard or whole-task spend.

Concrete paid-credit example: Astra with 10,000 uncached input tokens and 2,000 total billable output tokens costs about 5 credits Standard, 10 Fast, or 30 Ultrafast, calculated from the published rates. This excludes any additional tokens and tools; it is not an included-allowance estimate.

Whole-task time: tool calls, tests, waiting, and other overhead are not accelerated by the token-generation claim. An 8× token-generation rate does not guarantee an 8× faster coding task.

Course recommendation: use Standard for background work when waiting is inexpensive. Consider Fast or eligible Astra Ultrafast when you are actively waiting for the answer and the saved time justifies the higher usage.

How these rough ranges were constructed

These are historical API-equivalent estimates, not API credits or guaranteed current limits. Pro reports predate late-September policy changes; Plus evidence straddles that period. Four-week and 30-day projections are approximate. Check current plan usage rules before buying.
